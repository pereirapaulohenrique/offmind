V3 Variants

V3-A
Weight Contrast
Bold loop + bold "ff" + medium "mind". The weight shift creates natural reading hierarchy: off (concept) → mind (result). Like Focus where "fo" has more presence than "cus".
V3-B
Weight + Opacity
Same weight split, but "mind" also at 55% opacity. Stronger fade effect — "mind" recedes further. More dramatic hierarchy, closer to Focus's treatment.
V3-C
Color Split
Same weight throughout (Medium 500), but "off" is teal and "mind" is white. The loop and "ff" become one visual unit through shared color. Clean, minimal differentiation.
V3-D
Full Bold
All Bold 700, no split. The ONLY differentiation is the loop replacing "o". Simpler, more unified, more confident. The loop does all the conceptual work alone.

Focus Logo Lessons Applied

  • Symbol IS the letter: Loop sized to exact bold "o" dimensions — same baseline, x-height, and weight as Satoshi Bold.
  • Weight hierarchy: Bold "off" (concept carrier) vs Medium "mind" (the result). Eye reads the concept first.
  • Connected unit: Loop + "ff" share the same Bold weight, reading as one word fragment: "off".
  • Meaning encoded: "off" = what you do (offload). "mind" = what you get back (clarity). The weight shift mirrors this.

Variant Recommendation

  • V3-A (Weight Contrast) — Best balance. Bold "off" creates presence without being heavy-handed. Medium "mind" stays readable.
  • V3-C (Color Split) — Cleanest approach if you want same weight throughout. The teal "off" + white "mind" is elegant. But may not work in monochrome.
  • V3-B (Weight + Opacity) — Most dramatic. Works for hero sections but "mind" may be too faded for small sizes.
  • V3-D (Full Bold) — Simplest. If the loop alone carries enough meaning, you don't need weight/color splits. This is the "less is more" option.
  • Next step: Once you pick a direction, converting the text to outlined paths gives us full control over kerning, spacing, and the loop-to-letter transition.
